I Introduction  
\tRadar electronic jammers are developed from hostile countries, making them more complex and presenting severe issues when radar systems attempt to identify actual targets of interest. Jammers pose a formidable challenge in the realm of so-called digital radio frequency memories (DRFM). The working principle of DRFM technology involves generating coherent pseudo targets based on pulse signals intercepted by the radar transmitter and then producing these targets at the radar receiver. This process positions the pseudo targets either ahead or behind the real radar target, thereby masking the true target with the pseudo ones. Pseudo targets can also be manipulated in terms of amplitude, phase, and frequency.  
\tTraditional methods for detecting and estimating targets in electronic jammers typically rely on parameter modeling, which may fail due to its strict assumptions violated by classical signal processing algorithms. As a result, algorithm performance significantly drops. Moreover, designing a parameter model for handling DRFM interference is challenging, and it becomes ineffective against the evolving DRFM technology. A key opportunity to improve the protection and signal processing capabilities of electronic jammers in radar lies in leveraging machine learning techniques to challenge the fundamental assumptions of the standard parameter method used in radar system design and analysis.  
\tConvolutional neural networks (CNN) have become popular over the past few years as they leverage GPUs for optimal computational performance on faster high-performance computing systems. CNNs operate mathematically and are used for tasks such as image classification. When images are very similar to the training dataset, CNNs perform well in classifying images. However, little work has been done to establish realistic radar models that account for radar environment and antenna effects, leading to inaccurate simulation training datasets and reliability. Additionally, current publicly available research often does not consider the five dimensions of radar sensors, thus presenting a fragmented signal processing chain. From a first principles perspective, radar measurements target echoes in the following aspects: range, azimuth angle, elevation angle, Doppler, and signal amplitude.
